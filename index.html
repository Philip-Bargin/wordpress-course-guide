02

WordPress Installation & Initial Configuration

Option A — Using LocalWP (Recommended)

If using LocalWP, WordPress is already installed from Section 1. Proceed directly to Section 3.

Option B — One-Click Install via Hosting cPanel

Most hosts provide Softaculous or Installatron in their cPanel for one-click installs.

Step 2.1
Installing via cPanel (Live Hosting)
  1. Log in to your cPanel (usually at yourdomain.com/cpanel)
  2. Find "Softaculous Apps Installer" or "WordPress Manager"
  3. Click "Install" next to WordPress
  4. Fill in: Protocol (https://), Domain (sweetpalmbakery.com), leave Directory blank
  5. Site Name: Sweet Palm Bakery
  6. Site Description: Monrovia's Finest Artisan Bakery
  7. Admin Username: sweetpalm_admin   (never use "admin")
  8. Admin Password: strong password (20+ characters)
  9. Admin Email: owner@sweetpalmbakery.com
  10. Click "Install" and access at yourdomain.com/wp-admin
⚠️
Security Note

Never use admin as your WordPress username on a live site. It is the first username attackers try when brute-forcing login forms. Always choose a unique username during install.

5 Secrets to Our Famous Sweet Potato Rolls

Category: Baking Tips  |  Tags: sweet potato, bread baking, weekend specials, Liberian baking
Featured Image: sweet-potato-rolls.jpg
Excerpt: Every Saturday morning, a line forms outside our door for our legendary sweet potato rolls. Here are the 5 secrets that make them unforgettable.

Every Saturday morning, a line forms outside our door before we even flip the "Open" sign. The reason? Our legendary sweet potato rolls — pillowy, golden, and brushed with honey butter that melts the moment they come out of the oven. After years of customers begging for the recipe, we're sharing five secrets that make our rolls unforgettable.

Secret #1: We Roast, We Never Boil
Most bakeries use boiled or canned sweet potato. We never do. Roasting the sweet potatoes at 200°C concentrates their natural sugars, adds a subtle caramelized depth, and removes excess water. A drier potato makes a stronger, more flavorful dough.

Secret #2: Liberian Palm Oil in the Dough
A small measure of unrefined palm oil replaces some of the standard butter in our recipe. This gives our rolls a uniquely rich, nutty undertone that no other bakery in Monrovia can replicate. Palm oil also keeps the rolls soft for longer.

Secret #3: Two Rises Are Non-Negotiable
Impatient bakers cut the rise time short. We don't. Our dough rises for 90 minutes after mixing, then again for 45 minutes after shaping. Those two rises develop complex flavor and the tender, layered texture that makes you close your eyes on the first bite.

Secret #4: Honey Butter Glaze Goes On Immediately
Pull the rolls from the oven. Wait exactly 3 minutes. Then brush generously with honey butter while still warm. The glaze absorbs into the crust and creates a glistening, sweet shell that contrasts beautifully with the soft interior.

Secret #5: We Use Local Liberian Honey
We source raw forest honey from a small producer in Bong County. The floral, slightly smoky character of Liberian honey is unlike any commercially processed alternative. It makes every bite tell a story.

Come Taste the Difference
We bake fresh sweet potato rolls every Friday night for Saturday morning, and they typically sell out by 9:00 a.m. Visit us at 14 Tubman Boulevard, Sinkor, Monrovia, or call +231 777 123 456 to reserve yours. See you Saturday morning! 🍞

Post 3 — Cassava Bread Guide

📄 Post 3 — Full Sample Content

Everything You Never Knew About Liberian Cassava Bread

Category: Baking Tips  |  Tags: cassava, traditional baking, Liberian food, gluten-free
Featured Image: cassava-bread.jpg

Cassava bread is one of West Africa's oldest baked goods — and it may be the most underappreciated. Made from fermented cassava flour, it's naturally gluten-free, dense, and slightly sour in a way that pairs beautifully with palm butter soup or a cup of strong Liberian coffee.

What Is Cassava Bread?
Traditional cassava bread is made from bitter cassava that has been peeled, grated, pressed dry to remove toxic cyanide compounds, and then fermented. The result is a flour (called "gari" in many regional recipes) that is packed and baked over low heat into a flat, slightly chewy loaf.

Our Modern Take
At Sweet Palm, we honor the traditional method but add a few refinements: a longer fermentation (72 hours instead of 24), a blend of cassava flour and sorghum for structural support, and a natural honey glaze before the final bake. The result is a loaf that bridges the traditional and the contemporary without losing either.

Health Benefits
Cassava bread is 100% gluten-free · High in resistant starch (feeds healthy gut bacteria) · Low glycemic index compared to wheat bread · Rich in potassium and folate · Suitable for most allergy diets

WordPress Tools

Tools → Site Health

Go to Tools → Site Health. WordPress analyses your installation and reports issues in two tabs:

  • Status tab: Flags issues like outdated PHP versions, missing SSL, debug mode being ON, or failing scheduled events. Address all "Critical Issues" and as many "Recommendations" as possible.
  • Info tab: Detailed technical data — useful when contacting hosting support or troubleshooting plugin conflicts.

HTTPS / SSL CertificateActive (green padlock)
WordPress VersionLatest stable release
Debug ModeOFF on live sites
Scheduled Events (WP Cron)Running properly

Tools → Export Content

Step 16.2
Export Your Content as an XML File
  1. Go to Tools → Export
  2. Select "All content"
  3. Click "Download Export File"
  4. Save the .xml file — this is a portable backup of all posts, pages, comments, and settings
  5. This file can be imported into any other WordPress installation
💡
Note

The WordPress export/import tool is how you migrate content between hosts (e.g., from LocalWP to a live server). It exports text content, not media files — images must be migrated separately or re-downloaded by WordPress using the "Download and import file attachments" option during import.

Test Your Site Speed

  • Google PageSpeed Insights — pagespeed.web.dev — Target: 85+ on mobile, 90+ on desktop
  • GTmetrix — gtmetrix.com — Good for waterfall analysis of slow-loading resources
  • WebPageTest — webpagetest.org — Test from different geographic locations
💡
Note

In Liberia, a significant portion of users browse on mobile devices over mobile data connections. Optimising for mobile page speed is especially critical. Aim for pages that load in under 3 seconds on a typical 4G connection. The single biggest improvement is almost always image optimisation — compress all images with Smush before checking speed.
